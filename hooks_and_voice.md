# Hooks and voice: rules for every laowai reel (2026-11-11)

## Owner direction (Nov 11)
- The voice-over must have expression, not flat reading. Sometimes, when something is too crazy, react: "what the ****" with a censor bleep. Fun first.
- Never again: stories that romanticise an adult and a child (the quake rescue/marriage reel was pulled). Check every love story for age gaps that start with a minor.

## Hook research (Opus Clip analysis of clips; dev.to "Hook Engineering" 120-video test, self-reported numbers)
- People decide in ~1.5-2 s. First-3-second hold correlates ~0.84 with final views in one creator's test. Typical shorts keep 55-60% at 3 s; aim > 70%.
- Strongest archetypes: visual shock (+46%, risky if unrelated), curiosity gap (+42%, most reliable), pattern interrupt (+38%), bold claim (+35%), relatable pain (+31%, best on Reels). Curiosity gap + visual pattern interrupt together ~ +47%.
- Specific numbers beat round ones ("2,361 times", not "thousands").
- Hook in the very first frame: no intro, no logo, no greeting, no context first.
- Must work muted: on-screen text in the first second (68% of TikTok / 54% of Reels views start muted per a cited 2025 study).
- A sound cue inside 0.8 s acts as a pattern interrupt.
- Score each hook 0-20 on Clarity, Emotion, Disruption, Stakes, Promise; write 3+ variants, ship the best.

## Applying it
1. Line 1 = curiosity gap or bold claim with a specific number, spoken from frame 0, with the same words as on-screen text.
2. Frame 0 = the most absurd image of the story (motion, a face, or the impossible object), plus a sound hit at <= 0.5 s.
3. Open loop in the first 3 s that the ending pays off ("...and that wasn't even the crazy part").
4. Mid-video re-hooks every ~8-10 s: "But wait." / "Then it gets worse." / "Here's the catch."
5. One reaction beat per reel where the story earns it: a short interjection ("Wait. What?", "Nope.", "Bro.") or a bleeped "What the—[bleep]" (bleep covers the word fully; nothing explicit is audible or shown).
6. End on a question people can answer in one word.

## Voice: making it expressive
- Free (Kokoro): render each line separately, vary speed per line (setup 1.05-1.1, punchlines slower 0.95-1.0, lists faster 1.2), add 150-300 ms pauses before punchlines, pitch the reaction lines up 1-2 semitones, and use a second voice for the reaction beat so it sounds like a different person.
- Best (ElevenLabs Eleven v3, needs credits): audio tags such as [laughs], [gasps], [sighs], [whispers], [sarcastic], [excited] direct real emotion in the read. Not used yet: waiting for the owner's OK to spend credits.
