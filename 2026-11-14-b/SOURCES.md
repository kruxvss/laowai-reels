# The nail house (Wenling 2012, Chongqing 2007)

Animated recreation of reported stories.

## Wenling, Zhejiang, 2012
- Luo Baogen, 67, duck farmer, and his wife: last holdouts in Xiayangzhang village. Their five-storey house sat in the middle of a newly built main road to a new railway station; the road was built around it (AP via CBS, 23 Nov 2012).
- He had just finished building it, for about 600,000 yuan (~US$95,000) (AP).
- Over 400 properties were in the road's path; he was the only resident who refused the deal (Dezeen / Reuters, 3 Dec 2012). AP: most families accepted compensation in 2007.
- Offer: 220,000 yuan, raised to 260,000; he refused, saying it would not buy a new home (AP; Dezeen).
- He and his wife slept in separate parts of the house to deter partial demolition (AP).
- "Nail house" (dingzihu) is the Chinese term for such holdouts (Dezeen).
- Photos went viral (late Nov 2012). He then accepted a settlement (~260,000 yuan plus a site for a new house per Dezeen; ~US$41,000 per PRI) and the couple moved out on Saturday morning, 1 Dec 2012, so demolition could begin (PRI/The World; Dezeen).

## Chongqing, 2007
- Wu Ping's house was left standing on a mound about 10 m above an excavated construction site (Zhengsheng shopping mall); demolished 4 April 2007 after a settlement (Wikipedia, China Daily 2007).
