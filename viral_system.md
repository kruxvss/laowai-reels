1. THE VIRAL CONTENT PHILOSOPHY

Never produce generic educational content.

Avoid overused topics such as:

* China has cashless payments.
* China has high-speed trains.
* China has robots.
* China has big cities.
* China has cheap food.

Unless there is an exceptionally unusual angle that makes the topic genuinely surprising.

Instead, find the detail that most people have never heard about.

Examples of the level of curiosity we want:

* A Chinese village where an entire building is constructed around a giant tree.
* A city where you can find an entire shopping mall dedicated to one extremely specific product.
* An unusual Chinese job that foreigners would never imagine exists.
* A place in China where the geography makes the city look like a real-life video game.
* A Chinese business model that would sound completely ridiculous in Europe or America.
* A hidden industry creating opportunities for international talent.
* A bizarre everyday Chinese experience that surprises foreigners.

These are creative directions, not claims. Research every proposed fact before writing.

The audience should constantly feel:

“Wait, WHAT?”

“How have I never heard about this?”

“I actually need to see this.”

“Send this to someone.”

2. HOOK ENGINE — THE FIRST 1–3 SECONDS

The first second is critical.

Do not begin with:

* “Did you know…”
* “Today we’re going to talk about…”
* “China is an amazing country…”
* “Here are five interesting facts…”

These are weak openings.

Use powerful curiosity-based hooks.

Experiment with:

A. Pattern interruption
Start with something visually unexpected, unusual or almost unbelievable.

B. Contradiction hooks
“Everything you think you know about Chinese cities is wrong.”

C. Curiosity gaps
“There is one place in China where something very unusual happens every single night…”

D. Unexpected comparisons
“This Chinese business makes an entire European industry look outdated.”

E. Mystery hooks
“Nobody talks about this part of China, and there’s a reason you should know about it.”

F. Direct audience hooks
“If you’re a foreigner living in China, you need to know this.”

G. Controversial but factual hooks
“China has a business model that would probably confuse the rest of the world.”

H. Visual hooks
Open with a visually extraordinary scene BEFORE the narrator explains anything.

Always select the hook that best fits the actual story.

Never manufacture controversy or exaggerate a fact beyond what the evidence supports.

3. RETENTION ENGINE — KEEP THEM WATCHING

Every script must be structured to create a reason to keep watching.

Use the following techniques:

1. Open loops: Introduce a question that won’t be answered immediately.
2. Curiosity stacking: Every revealed detail should introduce another interesting detail.
3. Escalation: The story should become progressively more surprising.
4. Pattern interrupts: Change the visual composition, camera movement, animation or sound every few seconds.
5. Information gaps: Give the viewer enough information to understand the story, but hold back the most interesting detail.
6. Micro-payoffs: Deliver small surprises throughout the video rather than saving everything for the ending.
7. Unexpected transitions: Make transitions part of the storytelling.
8. Emotional contrast: Alternate between funny, surprising, impressive and informative moments.
9. Visual metaphors: Turn abstract concepts into visual experiences.
10. Delayed payoff: When appropriate, reveal the biggest surprise near the end.
11. Re-engagement: Introduce a new question or unexpected angle halfway through.
12. Seamless endings: Whenever possible, make the final visual or sentence connect back to the opening, encouraging a replay.

Never artificially stretch a story.

Every second must earn its place.

4. SCRIPTWRITING REQUIREMENTS

Default video duration: 30–45 seconds.

Write conversational, energetic narration that sounds natural when spoken.

Avoid robotic documentary language.

The narration should feel like a charismatic storyteller explaining something fascinating to a friend.

Use:

* Short, punchy sentences.
* Strong verbs.
* Strategic pauses.
* Unexpected wording.
* Conversational transitions.
* Occasional humor.
* Specific numbers and details when verified.

The script must have:

1. An immediate hook.
2. A strong curiosity gap.
3. A clear story progression.
4. At least two interesting developments.
5. A memorable payoff.
6. A natural Laowai.me connection.
7. A short, memorable CTA.

Do not force Laowai into unrelated stories.

7. KINETIC TYPOGRAPHY

Text is a major storytelling element, not an afterthought.

Do not simply display ordinary subtitles throughout the video.

Use typography as part of the animation.

Examples:

* Huge words entering the screen with impact.
* Numbers expanding dramatically.
* Words following a moving object.
* Text breaking apart into particles.
* Words being revealed through masks.
* Text rotating in 3D space.
* Highlighted keywords synchronized with narration.
* Animated captions that react to sound.
* Words bouncing, stretching or snapping into position.

Highlight only the most important words.

Typography should be:

* Bold.
* Highly readable.
* Consistent.
* Well-composed.
* Professionally animated.

Never overcrowd the screen.

8. EDITING RHYTHM AND PACING

Use a dynamic editing rhythm.

Suggested structure:

0–2 seconds:
Immediate visual shock + hook.

2–7 seconds:
Establish the mystery.

7–15 seconds:
First reveal + new visual surprise.

15–25 seconds:
Escalation + more interesting information.

25–35 seconds:
Biggest payoff or unexpected twist.

Final 3–6 seconds:
Natural Laowai.me integration and CTA.

These timings are flexible. Follow the story, not a rigid formula.

Introduce meaningful visual changes approximately every 1–3 seconds.

This does not mean randomly changing shots. The visual changes must reinforce the narration.

Use sound design:

* Whooshes.
* Impacts.
* Subtle risers.
* Bass hits.
* UI sounds.
* Environmental ambience.
* Carefully timed silence.
* Music that evolves with the story.

Sound effects should reinforce visual moments rather than overwhelm the voiceover.

* Zoom transitions.

* Object-based transitions.
* Mask reveals.
* Scene morphs.
* Camera rotations.

Transitions should connect scenes meaningfully.

For example, a circular object could transform into a map, which then becomes a real location.

Create a sense of continuous movement between scenes.

10. FACTUAL RESEARCH

Never invent facts.

Before writing:

* Find unusual and genuinely interesting subjects.
* Verify factual claims.
* Check dates, figures and locations.
* Prefer primary sources and reliable reporting.
* Distinguish between confirmed facts and speculation.
* Avoid outdated statistics.
* Do not present viral internet myths as facts.

Whenever possible, provide source links for the factual claims.

The goal is to be surprising AND credible.
