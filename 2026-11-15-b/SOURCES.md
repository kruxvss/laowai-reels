# The rooftop mountain villa (Beijing, 2013): research
Researched 2026-11-10.

## Facts and their sources
- Zhang Biqing founded a national chain of acupuncture/TCM clinics and was a former member of a district political advisory body. He lived on the top (26th) floor of a 26-storey residential tower. (AFP via Fox News 2013-08-16; PRI/The World 2013-08-14; AP via CBS 2013-08-13)
- Location: Park View estate, Haidian district, Beijing. (AP/CBS; SCMP)
- On the roof: a two-storey villa covered in fake rocks, trees and bushes, a "mountain". About 800 m2 (8,600 sq ft). (PRI; SCMP; AP/CBS)
- Six years of building; works began around 2007. (AP/CBS; AFP)
- Neighbours complained for years: damage to the building's structure and pipes, loud late-night parties, night-time renovations; authorities did not act. (AP/CBS)
- 12 Aug 2013: photos published in Chinese media went viral; officials then called it illegal. (AP/CBS; PRI)
- Haidian urban management gave him 15 days to demolish it himself or prove it was legal, or they would tear it down. (AP/CBS, official Dai Jun)
- He told the Beijing Times it was "just an ornamental garden", not a villa, and would comply. (AP/CBS)
- 15 Aug 2013: demolition started by workers he hired. About 50 workers on the job (Beijing Youth Daily via SCMP). Rubble could only go down in the lift after 10:30 pm. (SCMP)
- He said he spent 800,000 yuan on it and felt "pain in the heart". (SCMP) Not used in the script.

## Script choices
- "Officials give him fifteen days. Tear it down, or they will." follows the order (or prove it was legal; simplified).
- "He calls it just a garden" paraphrases "just an ornamental garden".
- "about fifty workers" hedged as reported.
- On-screen phone photo is a drawing; no like counts shown (no figures invented).
- Political-advisory role left out (not needed, avoids a political angle).
