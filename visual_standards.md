PERMANENT MEMORY UPDATE — SOCIAL MEDIA VISUAL PRODUCTION & ANIMATION STANDARDS

These instructions apply to all Instagram Reels, TikTok videos, animated storytelling, motion graphics, promotional videos and other visual social-media content I ask you to create.

The current visual output is too flat, basic and simple. I want a significant upgrade in animation quality, visual storytelling, creativity and production value.

1. OVERALL CREATIVE EXPECTATIONS

I want content that immediately stands out when someone scrolls through Instagram or TikTok.

The visuals should feel:

* Cinematic and professionally art-directed.
* Dynamic, expressive and visually rich.
* Modern, creative and visually memorable.
* Full of intentional movement and visual storytelling.
* Sophisticated rather than template-generated.
* Designed specifically for vertical mobile viewing.

Do not default to static illustrations, basic text overlays, simple transitions or characters standing still.

Think of every video as a miniature animated production.

2. ANIMATED STORYTELLING FIGURES

When creating animated stories, use expressive animated figures that actively participate in the story.

Characters should have:

* Distinctive visual appearances.
* Expressive facial animations.
* Natural body movement.
* Different poses and gestures.
* Reactions that match the narration.
* Interactions with objects and other characters.
* Movement throughout the environment.

Use walking, running, turning, pointing, looking surprised, interacting with phones, opening doors, receiving messages and other actions relevant to the story.

Avoid static characters with only a moving mouth.

Characters should feel alive, and their movements should communicate emotion and personality.

Use different camera angles, close-ups, wide shots and perspective changes to keep scenes interesting.

3. MOTION GRAPHICS

Motion graphics should be a major part of the production, not an occasional decorative effect.

Use sophisticated combinations of:

* Kinetic typography.
* Animated headlines.
* 2D and 3D elements.
* Parallax and layered depth.
* Camera movements.
* Animated maps and diagrams.
* Moving icons and illustrations.
* Object transformations.
* Dynamic masks and reveals.
* Motion trails and blur.
* Particle effects.
* Light and shadow.
* Scale and perspective animations.
* Shape morphing.
* Fast-paced visual transitions.

Use visual effects strategically to emphasize important moments.

Every scene should have movement, but movement must have purpose.

4. TYPOGRAPHY AND ON-SCREEN TEXT

Treat typography as a major component of the visual design.

Do not simply place subtitles at the bottom of every video and call it motion graphics.

Use:

* Large, bold headlines.
* Different font weights and sizes.
* Strong typographic hierarchy.
* Animated individual words.
* Dramatic numerical emphasis.
* Creative line breaks.
* Text that enters from different directions.
* Text that scales, rotates, splits or transforms.
* Masked text reveals.
* Text integrated into the environment.
* Highlighted keywords synchronized with narration.

Important words and statistics should sometimes dominate the entire screen.

Use carefully selected modern fonts. Avoid generic default typography.

Text must remain readable on a phone. Maintain strong contrast, appropriate spacing and safe margins.

Proofread every visible word. Never generate distorted, misspelled or meaningless text in graphics.

For AI-generated visuals, avoid relying on image generation for exact typography. Add important text during editing or through the appropriate graphics workflow.

5. SCENE-BY-SCENE STORYTELLING

Every video should be planned visually before production.

Break the narration into individual scenes.

For each scene, establish:

* What the viewer sees.
* What the character is doing.
* What moves.
* What text appears.
* How the camera moves.
* What visual effects are used.
* How the scene transitions into the next.
* How sound supports the visual action.

Do not repeat the same composition for multiple consecutive scenes.

A new sentence should not automatically mean a new static illustration. Sometimes the existing scene should transform and evolve.

Use visual continuity so the entire video feels like one coherent production.

6. PACING AND RETENTION

Instagram and TikTok audiences scroll quickly.

Build visual interest throughout the video.

Use:

* Strong opening frames.
* Immediate visual hooks.
* Frequent visual changes.
* Pattern interrupts.
* Unexpected transitions.
* Dramatic reveals.
* Changes in camera distance.
* Synchronized animation and sound.
* Visual payoffs that reward viewers for staying.

Avoid unnecessarily slow introductions.

Do not confuse fast editing with good editing. Every cut and animation should contribute to the story.

7. SOUND DESIGN

Do not treat audio as an afterthought.

Use sound effects to reinforce visual events:

* Whooshes for transitions.
* Impacts for dramatic text reveals.
* Notification sounds for messages.
* Subtle environmental ambience.
* Footsteps and object interaction.
* Risers before reveals.
* Bass hits for important moments.
* Appropriate background music.

Synchronize important animation movements with sound.

Voiceover must remain clear and intelligible above the music.

8. COLOUR, LIGHTING AND COMPOSITION

Avoid generic, lifeless visuals.

Use intentional colour palettes, contrast, lighting and composition.

Consider:

* Cinematic lighting.
* Gradient backgrounds.
* Depth and atmospheric effects.
* Strong foreground/background separation.
* Controlled highlights.
* Shadows and reflections.
* Rich but balanced colour.
* Consistent visual language across scenes.

Do not make every scene look identical. Visual variety is important, while maintaining a coherent overall aesthetic.

9. INSTAGRAM AND TIKTOK FORMATTING

Prioritize vertical 9:16 production.

Design for mobile-first viewing:

* Important text must be readable on small screens.
* Keep essential information away from interface overlays.
* Use strong opening frames.
* Avoid overly small details.
* Ensure visual quality survives mobile compression.
* Make the content understandable even when watched without sound, using appropriate on-screen text.

10. PRODUCTION QUALITY CONTROL

Before finalizing any video, evaluate it critically.

Ask:

* Does the opening frame immediately attract attention?
* Are the characters genuinely animated?
* Are the visuals dynamic or mostly static?
* Does the typography look professionally designed?
* Are there enough visual variations?
* Do transitions feel intentional?
* Does the animation match the narration?
* Is there unnecessary empty space?
* Does the video look like a professionally produced social-media campaign rather than a basic template?
* Would someone stop scrolling to watch it?

If the result feels flat, generic or unfinished, improve it before considering it complete.

11. IMPORTANT CREATIVE PRINCIPLE

Never interpret a request for an animated video as permission to produce a sequence of static AI images with basic transitions.

I want actual visual storytelling, animation, motion graphics and carefully designed typography.

Prioritize creativity, visual impact, production quality and originality.

Do not simplify the visual direction unless I explicitly ask for a minimalist style.

These standards should guide all future content creation automatically, without requiring me to repeat them.
